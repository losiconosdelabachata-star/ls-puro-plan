Brand identity & market positioning

A cigar house built on composition

The established houses sell tobacco dynasties or Swiss precision. LS PURO can own a third idea that none of them has: authorship. Each blend is composed the way a song is composed, and signed with the guitar pick.

What makes LS PURO different

  • A real author, not an endorsement. Lenny Santos is presented as the composer of each line, with a named master blender and factory beside him. The celebrity opens the door; the blend and the factory earn the reorder.
  • A complete ritual at launch. Cigar, cutter, lighter, ashtray and travel case under one mark from day one. Few new cigar brands arrive with a priced accessory collection already designed. Verified
  • A cultural root no competitor holds. Bachata is Dominican music; the Dominican Republic is also one of the great cigar origins. If the factory is Dominican (to be confirmed), the story writes itself honestly.
  • A musical naming system. Perfect Melody, Legend Blend and Don Marino already read as movements of one work. Keep extending it: vitolas named for tempos or song forms, limited releases as “editions”.

Unique selling proposition

“Luxury cigars composed like music: developed with a named master blender, blind-tested before release, and finished with a complete ritual under one signature.”

Guardrail. Retailers have watched celebrity cigars arrive with noise and fade within a year. Every brand message should lead with the cigar (factory, leaf, consistency, ratings) and let the name follow. Never use the Aventura name or marks without written rights. Attorney

Personality, voice and the ideal customer

Personality

Composed, unhurried, exacting, warm. Like a musician talking about craft after the show: few words, chosen carefully. Bilingual where it is natural, never forced.

Avoid: nightclub imagery, party language, bottle-service flash, anything that reads young.

Voice in practice

Say: “Rested four months before it leaves the factory.” “Every box color-sorted by hand.”

Don't say: “The smoothest cigar ever.” “Lit.” Health-adjacent words like “natural”, “clean” or “mild” without counsel review. Attorney

Ideal customers (21+ only)

  • The Connoisseur: buys boxes, follows ratings, needs proof of quality.
  • The Collector & Gifter: buys limited editions and accessories for milestones.
  • The Culture Loyalist: an established Latino professional who grew up on bachata, often newer to premium cigars and open to guidance.

Personas are planning archetypes, not measured demographics. Assumption

Development roadmap with approval gates Recommendation

  1. Manufacturer due diligenceVisit the factory. Confirm capacity, current brands produced, export history, minimums, lead times, pricing, and who owns the blend recipe (LS PURO should, by contract). Ask three retailers who carry that factory's brands about consistency and on-time delivery.
  2. Blend brief for each lineStrength target, flavor direction, price tier and vitolas, written as a one-page “composition brief”. Perfect Melody, Legend Blend and Don Marino should be clearly different experiences, not three bands on one blend.
  3. Prototype roundsThree to five blend candidates per line. Every round goes through the blind panel below and is scored against a benchmark cigar at the same price.
  4. Pre-production samples in final packagingBands, cellophane decision, box, humidity control. Rest the cigars for the period the factory specifies, then re-test. Tobacco changes after rolling; approve what the customer will actually smoke.
  5. Golden sample and pilot runSeal and store a signed “golden sample” box of each SKU at LS PURO and at the factory. Run a pilot lot and test three separate lots for consistency.
  6. Commercial production with lot-level QAEvery lot coded, sampled, smoke-tested and recorded. Keep a retained-sample library from every lot.

Quality standards to write into the supply agreement

Construction

Even fill with no soft spots or hard knots; clean triple or seamless cap; smooth wrapper with minimal veins; bands placed at one height; boxes color-sorted.

Draw

Every cigar machine-tested for draw resistance at the factory, with an agreed acceptable range. Tight or “plugged” cigars rejected before boxing.

Combustion

Even burn line, few touch-ups, firm ash, and stays lit through a short pause. Measured on a set number of cigars per lot.

Storage

Commonly kept around 65–70% relative humidity and 65–70°F; heat above the mid-70s raises beetle risk. Confirm the factory's own targets and print care guidance in every box. Industry norm

Batch testing

Lot code on every box. Pull a fixed sample per 1,000 for weight, size and draw; smoke-test a smaller sample; keep retained samples from every lot.

Independent evaluation

Blind panel before launch (protocol below). After launch, ratings follow retail presence: Cigar Aficionado rates blind and buys most of the cigars it rates at retail. Publish scores only as received. source

Blind testing protocol Recommendation

  1. Panel8–12 evaluators, all 21+: at least three tobacconists or lounge managers, three experienced smokers (10+ years), one or two industry judges, and the blender.
  2. Blind codingBands removed, cigars coded A–D, with one benchmark cigar from an established brand at the same price.
  3. ConditionsCigars rested at least two weeks at a controlled humidity, same cut, same lighter type, water only, one cigar per sitting.
  4. Decision ruleRelease when the blend scores at or above the benchmark and construction faults stay under the agreed threshold. Repeat on three production lots.

Direct-to-consumer economics

The appeal

Selling direct captures the retail margin, roughly double the brand's wholesale revenue per cigar, and builds a customer list LS PURO owns.

The catch

Online cigar sales bring state tax and licensing duties, adult-signature delivery, shipping restrictions in some states, payment-processor limits on tobacco, and conflict with the retailers you are asking for shelf space. Attorney

Recommendation: sell accessories direct from day one. For cigars, launch with a “find a partner humidor” locator and add direct sales state by state only after counsel signs off, always at full MSRP so partners are never undercut. Recommendation

05

Retailer & distributor acquisition

Earning the shelf, one humidor at a time

Premium cigars are sold by people: the owner who decides what goes in the walk-in, and the staff who hand a box to a customer. The first 20 accounts matter more than the next 200, so the plan is selective, personal and measured.

Step by step, from target list to reorder

  1. Define the ideal account
    Walk-in humidor with ultra-premium brands already on the shelf, a lounge or event space, an owner who hosts events, and a customer base that includes established Latino professionals. Score every prospect on these five points.
  2. Build the target list
    Start in 2–3 home markets with both bachata audiences and strong cigar lounges, such as South Florida, Texas and the New York–New Jersey area. State tax shapes the shelf price: Florida excludes cigars from its other-tobacco tax, Texas taxes cigars by the thousand, and New York taxes them at 75% of wholesale (section 09). Sources: Premium Cigar Association member listings, the store locators of comparable luxury brands, cigar media retailer guides, and local scouting visits. Tier the list: A (15–25 flagship lounges), B (premium shops), C (later).
  3. Warm the approach
    Visit as a customer first. Buy something, learn the staff's names, see what sells. Then send the introduction letter (below) addressed to the owner by name.
  4. Secure the meeting
    Call five business days after the letter. Ask for 30 minutes in the shop, at a quiet hour, with the humidor manager present.
  5. Present like a luxury house
    A walnut presentation box with the three lines, one accessory set, the printed catalog and a one-page price sheet. Follow the 12-part presentation in section 11. Never open with the celebrity; open with the cigar.
  6. Prove the quality
    Offer a blind evaluation: coded LS PURO cigars plus a benchmark the owner chooses from their own humidor. Share the factory credentials and the pre-launch panel scores. Where trade samples are allowed (see section 09), leave a coded kit for staff.
  7. Make a clear opening offer
    The Launch Partner package below: small enough to say yes to, complete enough to merchandise properly.
  8. Negotiate on support, not price
    Hold MSRP and wholesale. Trade instead on event dates, display, staff education, limited-release allocation and a slow-mover exchange.
  9. Engineer the reorder
    Train staff within 7 days of delivery, host the launch evening within 30, call at day 30, review sell-through at day 60 with a suggested reorder, and run a business review at day 90.
  10. Keep the relationship
    Allocate limited releases by sell-through and loyalty, not by order size. Visit A accounts monthly. Feature partner stores on the LS PURO locator.

Legal, regulatory & compliance roadmap

The work that comes before the first sale

Premium cigar regulation in the United States has been changed by litigation in recent years and differs by state. This is a research briefing to organize the work with a tobacco regulatory attorney. It is not legal advice, and no product should be marketed on the assumption that a manufacturer's existing operations cover a new brand.

The federal position today Verified

A federal court vacated FDA's 2016 “deeming rule” as it applies to premium cigars (August 2023). The D.C. Circuit largely affirmed in January 2025, and the district court finalized the definition in April 2026. No later appeal was found, though the docket could not be checked directly. source

In practice: an LS PURO cigar that meets all eight criteria needs no FDA premarket authorization, establishment registration, product listing or ingredient listing. Any SKU that misses one criterion stays under FDA rules and needs premarket authorization before sale. Attorney

Still open: FDA may start new rulemaking, and bills that would write the exemption into law (H.R. 2111, S. 3922) have not passed. FDA says it intends not to charge premium cigars user fees but still sends invoices, so a new importer may need to dispute one. In flux

Every SKU must meet all eight

  1. Wrapped in whole tobacco leaf
  2. Binder of 100% leaf tobacco
  3. At least 50% long filler by weight
  4. Made by hand or hand rolled
  5. No filter, non-tobacco tip or mouthpiece
  6. No characterizing flavor other than tobacco
  7. Contains only tobacco, water and vegetable gum
  8. Weighs more than 6 pounds per 1,000 cigars

These line up with the manufacturer specifications in section 02. Get each one certified in writing for every SKU.

Executive recommendations

The verdict

LS PURO has rare assets for a new cigar brand: a name with real reach, a coherent luxury look, and a finished accessory line. What it does not yet have is the thing retailers buy: a proven cigar from a named factory, with the permits and paperwork behind it. Close that gap first, and launch small and excellent.

Five biggest opportunities

  1. Built-in demand from a global bachata audience that no cigar house speaks to.
  2. Accessories as an early, lower-regulation revenue stream with 55–60% retailer margins already priced.
  3. A distinctive, ownable story: composition and authorship, with a ready naming system.
  4. Lounge events as the growth engine: a name that fills a room for partner retailers.
  5. Limited, numbered releases that build collectors and allocation leverage.

Five biggest risks

  1. The cigar underdelivers and the brand is filed as a celebrity novelty.
  2. Regulatory missteps on permits, labels, sampling or advertising, especially reaching under-21 fans.
  3. Rights gaps: name-and-likeness licence, trademark clearance for PURO, Aventura references, band art.
  4. Cash strain and cost shocks from inventory, the 12.5% import duty in force since July 2026, excise tax and launch spend before reorders arrive.
  5. Inconsistent supply from an unproven factory, or blend ownership left with the factory.

First steps, in order

  1. Engage a tobacco regulatory attorney.
  2. Sign the name-and-likeness licence; file trademarks.
  3. Choose the manufacturer; sign a supply agreement that gives LS PURO the blend.
  4. Complete the blend rounds and blind panel.
  5. Set the price tier with real quotes in the section 04 model.

Before the first production run

  • Golden samples approved and sealed
  • All spec sheets and declarations in hand
  • Packaging statements cleared by counsel
  • Permits for importer and brand roles in place
  • Order sized to the target account list

Before approaching retailers

  • Production stock landed and stored properly
  • Price sheet, catalog and presentation printed
  • Blind-panel results documented
  • Age-gated site and locator live
  • Event calendar and Lenny's availability confirmed

The most cost-effective entry that keeps the luxury intact

Start with one market, one or two lines, and a handful of humidors. Launch Perfect Melody and Legend Blend (their bands already exist) in two vitolas each, with 15–20 flagship partners in a single metro area (South Florida is the strongest candidate: no state other-tobacco tax on cigars, a large Latino community and a deep lounge culture), sold direct by the founder team with accessories alongside. Spend on the cigar, the box and the in-person evenings, not on advertising. Hold MSRP. Add Don Marino, a distributor and the trade-show debut only after 90 days of reorder data. Scarcity reads as luxury; a thin national rollout reads as a celebrity novelty. Recommendation

A cigar's size and shape, such as a Robusto. Written as length × ring gauge, for example 5 × 50.
Ring gauge
Diameter in 64ths of an inch. A 50 ring gauge is 50/64 of an inch across.
Wrapper · binder · filler
The outer leaf (capa), the leaf holding the bunch together (capote), and the leaves inside (tripa).
Long filler
Whole leaves running the length of the cigar, the mark of a premium handmade cigar.
Puro
Spanish for cigar; in the trade, also a cigar whose leaves all come from one country.
Draw
How easily air passes through a lit cigar. A “plugged” cigar barely draws.
Priming
The position of a leaf on the plant; higher primings are usually stronger.
Keystone
A retail price set at double the wholesale cost, about a 50% retailer margin.
MSRP · MAP
Manufacturer's suggested retail price; minimum advertised price policy.
FET · OTP
Federal excise tax; “other tobacco products”, the state tax category that usually covers cigars.
Landed cost
The full cost of a cigar delivered to the U.S. warehouse, taxes and freight included.
Sell-through
How much of what a retailer bought has actually sold to customers.
Allocation
Rationing limited cigars among retailers, usually rewarding loyal, high-sell-through accounts.
